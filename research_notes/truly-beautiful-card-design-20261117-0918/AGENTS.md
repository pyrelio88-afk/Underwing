Mission (verbatim, as assigned by the requesting agent):

> Research question: what makes a card design TRULY beautiful and effective — not generic "nice UI", but the specific craft behind the best-looking cards in the world — and how do those principles apply to a Chinese-language home-care instruction card web app?
> 
> Context for applying the findings (do not treat as a request to build anything): a single-file static web app. Each card teaches ONE home-care task (e.g. measuring blood pressure, fall prevention, recognizing low blood sugar) to first-time family caregivers around 40 years old caring for elderly parents. Users are anxious, non-medical, reading on desktop first and phones second. Cards must look genuinely premium ("超级好看") while staying calm, trustworthy and readable — never flashy or gamey. Constraints: red is reserved ONLY for "stop and contact medical help" warnings; illustration style is soft flat vector (matte muted color blocks, minimal outlines, warm cream background); UI uses mist-grey background, white cards with thin 1px lines, dark-green primary buttons, fast transitions; cards may use tasteful 3D tilt/parallax/glare but must respect prefers-reduced-motion; text must stay large and high-contrast for older readers.
> 
> Research these five areas with real sources (design teardowns, official style guides, GDC/game-art talks, accessibility standards, award-winning app case studies — cite each claim):
> 1. Trading card games (Pokémon TCG, Magic: The Gathering, Hearthstone, Yu-Gi-Oh!, Marvel Snap, One Piece Card Game, Disney Lorcana): card anatomy and layout grid, visual hierarchy order (what the eye hits first/second/last), frame/border design, art-window proportions and how art meets frame, typography in rules text, rarity/foil systems and how they signal value without noise, color-coding by type, icon systems, and what each game does DIFFERENTLY that works. Include how modern TCGs evolved their frames over editions and why.
> 2. Digital product card UI at its best (iOS design patterns, Material Design cards, award-winning health/education/finance apps, Awwwards-level card layouts): spacing rhythm, corner radius, shadow vs border, image-to-text ratio, information density limits, card grids vs lists, and what separates a premium-feeling card from a generic "AI template" card.
> 3. Readability and trust for anxious non-expert adults and older readers: type sizes/weights/line-height, contrast minimums (WCAG), scannable structure (progressive disclosure, front/back of card), how to make warnings unmissable without frightening, and evidence on illustration vs photo vs icon for instructional content.
> 4. Illustration-in-card craft: how flat illustration and card frames harmonize (background continuity, art window shape, bleed vs contained art), consistent illustration systems across a deck (palette discipline, recurring characters/props, composition templates), and common failures (art fighting the frame, muddy thumbnails, inconsistent lighting).
> 5. Motion on cards: tasteful tilt/parallax/glare implementations (what angles, speeds, and light behavior feel premium vs gimmicky), flip animations for card front/back, and reduced-motion best practice.
> 
> Deliverable: a sourced report in Chinese, organized as (a) the 10-15 highest-impact design rules ranked by impact, each with WHY it works and a concrete example; (b) a "do NOT do" list of common card-design failures with reasons; (c) a recommended card anatomy (front and back) for this care-card app, with layout proportions and hierarchy order; (d) open questions where sources disagree or evidence is thin. Distinguish verified facts from opinions. Include source URLs for every major claim.
